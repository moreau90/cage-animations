Why the revert-to-sliders plan is a trap

That diff you showed does two bad things:

It throws away the cage-derived shoulder center + arm axis, which were solving asymmetry and giving you real references.

It locks your arm direction to pure ±X again, which is almost never true once the character isn’t perfectly aligned — and it’s exactly why the elbow/wrist pivots started drifting.

It might make one pose look “less stretched,” but it will not solve bicep crinkling, and it will bring back weird edge cases.

The plan that actually fixes bicep crinkling in your architecture
Do these two changes, keep everything cage-derived:
Fix A — Geometry-based “is this an arm vertex?” detection

Inside buildBindKNNRegion, before deciding allowedRegions, compute:

projection tL and tR onto left/right arm axis (using cage-derived shoulderJoint + armDir)

distance to arm axis (point-to-line distance)

If the vertex is close to the arm axis and within an arm-length range, treat it as an arm vertex even if meshRegion came back as torso.

Then apply the torso clamp to those vertices.

This directly addresses “bicep verts classified as torso.”

Fix B — Clamp torso influence past a shoulder band

For vertices detected as “upperarm-ish”:

if t > band → disallow torso (0)

else allow it

And don’t hardcode band=0.04. Tie it to upperArmLen:

band = 0.15 * upperArmLen (start)

Optional nuclear option: hard seam

Hard seam (no blending) can work if your transforms are truly continuous (upperarm → forearm composed). But it’s usually a last resort because you’ll trade “crinkle” for “visible crease.”
